Why retire abroad — and why Florianópolis

The pros that draw retirees to the Island of Magic

Retiring abroad is not for everyone, but for many people it opens the door to a fuller, calmer, and more affordable chapter of life. Here is what makes Florianópolis stand out.

Your dollar can stretch further

The cost of living in Brazil is generally lower than in most of the United States, and a favorable exchange rate can help your savings and retirement income go further on everyday expenses, dining, and housing. Actual costs vary by lifestyle and are never guaranteed.

A stunning island coastline

Florianópolis is an island city famous for its natural beauty — more than 40 beaches, along with lagoons, dunes, and forested hills. Whether you love calm bays or open surf, the ocean is never far.

High quality of life

Florianópolis is consistently regarded as one of Brazil’s best cities for quality of life, and is often noted for feeling safer and calmer than many other major Brazilian cities. It draws people looking for a relaxed, livable pace.

Warm climate & outdoor life

A subtropical climate means mild winters and long, sunny summers. Days fill easily with surfing, sailing, hiking coastal trails, and fresh seafood — an outdoor lifestyle that is hard to give up once you try it.

Culture & cuisine

The island carries a rich Azorean heritage, a relaxed pace, and a welcoming community. Local kitchens celebrate fresh seafood, and the food culture rewards anyone who loves eating well and simply.

Healthcare access

Brazil has both a public health system and a large private healthcare sector. Major cities like Florianópolis are home to private hospitals and clinics, giving residents options for care. This is shared as general information, not medical or insurance advice.

Attainable coastal real estate

Compared with many US and European coastal markets, oceanfront and near-water property in Florianópolis is often more attainable. It is a chance to own near the water without a big-city coastal price tag — though values vary and nothing is guaranteed.

A growing expat community

Florianópolis has an expanding international and expat community, which makes settling in easier — from finding English-speaking neighbors to sharing tips on daily life, services, and the best corners of the island.

Peace of mind

Safe, welcoming, and easier than you think

The three questions Americans ask most — is it safe, will I manage the language, and will I find community? Here is the honest picture.

Among Brazil’s safest cities

Florianópolis consistently ranks as one of the safest state capitals in Brazil, with a high quality of life and Human Development Index. Violent crime is relatively rare, especially in the beach neighborhoods. As anywhere, use normal city sense with petty theft (phones, car break-ins) in busy tourist areas.

You can get by in English

Portuguese is the local language, but English is fairly widespread in tourist and expat areas — younger people, restaurant staff, and coworking managers often speak it. You will settle in faster by picking up some Portuguese, and we can point you to English-friendly services and professionals.

A real international community

The island has a strong, growing community of expats, remote workers, and digital nomads — it is regularly rated one of South America’s top cities for foreigners. You will not be starting from scratch or going it alone.

Sources: Numbeo, Travel Safe Abroad, and expat guides (2026). Conditions vary by neighborhood and season; general information, not a guarantee of safety.

Healthcare

Quality care, a fraction of US prices

Brazil has both a free public system and an affordable private sector — and Florianópolis is known for well-run hospitals. Here is how it works.

Free public system (SUS)

Available to all legal residents, including foreigners — doctor visits, surgeries, and prescriptions at no cost. Florianópolis runs it better than most, with urgent-care UPAs and a university hospital. Trade-off: longer waits for specialists.

Private insurance: ~$150–$350/mo

Plans from Unimed, Bradesco Saúde, or SulAmérica cost roughly $150–$350 per month in Florianópolis, depending on age and coverage — a fraction of US premiums, with short waits and English-friendly scheduling.

Pay-as-you-go is cheap too

A private GP visit runs about $45–$85; a specialist about $65–$120. Many retirees mix public care, private insurance, and out-of-pocket as it suits them.

Strong private hospitals

Expats often use Hospital Baía Sul (robotic surgery, broad specialties) and Hospital SOS Cárdio (internationally certified, cardiology & emergency care).

English-speaking doctors are available in the private system but not universal — private insurance helps. Costs vary by age, plan, and provider. Sources: Pacific Prime, Rio Times, expat guides (2026). General information, not medical or financial advice.

Common questions

Retiring in Brazil: your questions, answered

Can I own property in Brazil as a foreigner?

Yes. Foreigners can buy and own property in Brazil with essentially the same rights as citizens. You will need a CPF (Brazilian tax number), a local bank account, and a legal representative for the deed. (Some rural/border-zone land has restrictions, but typical urban and coastal homes do not.)

How much income do I need for a retirement visa?

Preliminary research indicates around US$2,000 per month or more in retirement/pension income. Exact thresholds and dependent rules vary and change over time — our immigration attorney confirms what applies to your case.

Can I still receive my US Social Security in Brazil?

Generally yes — the US Social Security Administration pays benefits to eligible US citizens living in Brazil, usually by direct deposit. Confirm your specific situation with the SSA. (General information, not financial advice.)

Will I owe taxes?

US citizens keep filing US taxes on worldwide income no matter where they live, and Brazil taxes its tax-residents on worldwide income once you become one. It is manageable but individual — plan with a cross-border tax professional. (Not tax advice.)

Do I need to speak Portuguese?

Not to get started — English gets you by in expat and tourist areas — but learning some Portuguese makes daily life much easier and richer. We can point you to English-friendly services and professionals.

Can I bring my spouse, dependents, or pets?

Typically yes. Visas can include dependents (income requirements may be higher), and pets can be brought with the right veterinary paperwork. The attorney handles the specifics for your family.

These answers are general information, not legal, tax, immigration, or financial advice. Rules change and vary by person — we connect you with qualified professionals to confirm your situation.
