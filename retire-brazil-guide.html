Free Guide · Florianópolis, SC, Brazil

How Americans retire in Florianópolis — on about $2,000 a month

A plain-English guide to retiring on Brazil’s “Island of Magic”: what it costs, how the visa works, how to buy a home, and how we handle the whole move for you. Want a personal plan? Talk to us any time.

The money

Can you really retire on ~$2,000 a month?

For many Americans, yes — Brazil’s cost of living commonly runs 40–60% below the US, so a Social Security or pension check that feels tight at home can stretch into a comfortable coastal lifestyle.

Your exact budget depends on the neighborhood, whether you own or rent, and your lifestyle — but the headline is simple: a favorable exchange rate plus lower everyday costs (groceries, dining, healthcare, help around the home) means your dollars go further in Florianópolis than in most of Florida.

  • Everyday costs — food, transport, utilities — are typically a fraction of US prices.
  • Private healthcare is accessible and far more affordable than in the US.
  • Owning your home (see below) removes rent from the equation entirely.

Figures are general and vary by person, neighborhood, and exchange rate. This is not financial advice — we will help you build real numbers for your situation.

The visa

Living in Brazil, legally

Preliminary research shows retirees may qualify to live in Brazil on a retirement or pension income of about US$2,000 per month or more — and the whole process is handled by professionals we trust.

  • Permanent residency: Bergamasco Advogados (immigration & real estate attorney João Henrique Bergamasco) handles permanent visas and residency for Americans.
  • Just visiting first? Brazil Connect (Thaysa M. Gomes) can process a tourist visa — often in days.

Immigration rules, income thresholds, and dependent rules vary and change over time. This is general information, not legal or immigration advice — the attorney confirms exactly what applies to you.

Your home

Buying property as a foreigner

Good news: foreigners can buy property in Florianópolis with essentially the same rights as Brazilian citizens. The process is one of the more straightforward in the region.

  • You will need a CPF (Brazilian tax number), a local bank account, and a qualified legal representative for the deed transfer.
  • Jurêrê Internacional is the benchmark for luxury; other neighborhoods offer a range of price points.
  • Our local Realtor partner, Jucileide Polli Piegas, guides you through neighborhoods, pricing, and the buying process on the ground.
